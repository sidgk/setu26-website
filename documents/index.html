Reports and documents

Reports reach the patient. Their old reports reach your doctor.

Setu26 ties every document to the right patient record and moves it in both directions: from your hospital to the patient's app or phone, and from the patient to your hospital when they choose.
Ask about document sharingHow it works
In pilotBuilt and tested end to end, and offered to pilot hospitals. Not yet used by a hospital with real patients.

From the hospital to the patient

In pilot
  1. 1

    Staff choose the patient and the document

    A lab or scan report, discharge summary, certificate, a prescription (typed or dictated) or a bill. A check box confirms it is the right patient.

  2. 2

    It is added to that patient's record

    Stored privately in India. Files are checked by their contents and size before they are accepted.

  3. 3

    The patient receives it

    In their Swasthya app, or as an SMS link that opens only with their date of birth and expires in 7 days.

  4. 4

    Staff see what happened

    In the app, notified, opened. Resend, correct with a new version, or withdraw; the patient is told.

From the patient to the hospital

In pilot
  1. 1

    The patient adds their documents

    Photos, scans or PDFs of earlier reports, in Swasthya. They are always labelled as uploaded by the patient.

  2. 2

    They choose what to share, and for how long

    Specific documents with one connected hospital, or time-limited access to their history. They can stop it at any time.

  3. 3

    The hospital can ask

    Staff can request a document or history access; the patient decides.

  4. 4

    Staff review it

    Shared documents appear under Shared with us and on the patient's record. Opening one is recorded; staff mark it reviewed.

Supported documents

The documents a hospital sends every day.

As PDF, photo or scan (JPEG, PNG, WebP, HEIC, TIFF, JPEG 2000, DICOM), up to 30 MB for PDF and DICOM and 25 MB for photos.
  • Lab reports
  • Scan and radiology reports
  • Discharge summaries
  • Consultation notes
  • Prescriptions (typed or dictated)
  • Bills and payment receipts
  • Medical certificates
  • Referral letters
  • Vaccination records
  • Insurance papers

Access and records

Who can see what, and how it is recorded.

Only staff who need it

Sending documents and reading what patients shared are separate permissions, given by role. A receptionist can send a report without being able to read a patient's shared history.

Patients decide what they share

A patient shares chosen documents with one hospital, or gives time-limited access to their history after confirming it is them. They see who can see what, and can stop it at any time.

Every opening is recorded

Issuing, opening, refusals and changes are written to the hospital's audit log. Patients see when their emergency card was viewed.

Right patient, right file

Staff confirm they picked the right patient before sending. Files are checked by their actual contents and size; damaged or password-protected PDFs are refused.

Links that protect themselves

For patients without the app, the SMS link opens only with their date of birth, expires after 7 days, stops after 5 wrong tries or 10 opens, and is rate-limited.

Stored in India

Files are kept privately in Google Cloud's Mumbai region, with a fingerprint (SHA-256) of each version. Corrections add a new version; the old one is kept.

What it is not

  • Not an electronic medical record: it moves documents between a hospital and its patients; it does not replace your clinical records.
  • It does not read or interpret reports, and gives no medical advice.
  • Not connected to ABDM or ABHA, or to other hospitals' systems.
  • Not end-to-end encrypted: connections and storage are encrypted, and access is controlled and recorded as described above.
  • Uploads are checked by type and size; they are not scanned for viruses yet.
  • SMS links reach patients once a hospital's SMS sending is switched on; in our test hospital they go to a test inbox.
